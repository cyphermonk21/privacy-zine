# Design Principles

## Philosophy

This zine exists because privacy shouldn't require a computer science degree. The goal is a pocket-sized reference that someone can glance at, recognize a service they use, and immediately see a privacy-respecting alternative.

## Design Decisions

### Side-by-Side Comparison
Privacy tools appear on one side, mainstream equivalents on the other. This isn't about shaming — it's about making the invisible visible. People often don't realize how many Google services they touch daily.

### "say / see / buy / believe"
These verbs frame the four fundamental human actions that platforms monetize:
- **say** — your communications
- **see** — what you browse
- **buy** — your purchasing behaviour
- **believe** — the information that shapes your worldview

### Minimal Text
The zine is meant to be scannable in under 60 seconds. Detail lives on the website (accessed via QR code), not on the page.

### Printable
If it can't be printed on a home printer and handed to someone, it fails its purpose.

## Tool Selection Criteria

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for the full inclusion/exclusion criteria.

### Category Limits
Each category includes 2–4 privacy tools maximum. More isn't better — it's overwhelming. The landing page has room for expanded lists.

## Visual Identity
- High contrast for readability
- No dependence on color alone (grayscale must remain legible)
- Icons should be recognizable at small print sizes
